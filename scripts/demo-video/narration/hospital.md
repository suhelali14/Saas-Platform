# Hospital plan — narration

Reads on top of the Clinic and Polyclinic videos. Everything those two
plans do still works; this walks the viewer through the desks a small
hospital adds.

---

**[INTRO]**  
The Hospital plan. Everything the Clinic and Polyclinic plans do — the
queue, the consultation, the patient record, the bill, the pharmacy,
the website — still works. This is what a small hospital adds: the
desks a hospital day runs on.

**[SECTION 1 — Departments]**

**Departments.**  
Clinical departments on top — medicine, surgery, paediatrics,
gynaecology. Service points below — the front desk, the cash counter,
insurance, schemes, pharmacy, laboratory, radiology, theatre, wards.
Assign a person to a desk and they see that desk's screen in their menu.

**[SECTION 2 — The cash counter]**

**Opening the counter.**  
The cashier starts the day. Opening cash in the drawer, counter name.
From this moment, every rupee they take belongs to this session.

**Advances and payments.**  
A patient pays an advance, or settles a bill. Only the cashier who
opened the counter takes money on it. Every receipt belongs to a shift
that can be counted at close.

**[SECTION 3 — Inpatients]**

**Bed board.**  
Every ward, every bed. Taken beds show the patient, the admitting
doctor, how long they have been in. Free beds admit from here.

**One admission.**  
Charges, room days, the deposit, the running bill. The family can see
exactly what they are being billed for, and the ward can top up when
the deposit runs short.

**Discharge.**  
At discharge the account becomes one final bill, with room days and
every charge on it. Not three invoices at the door.

**[SECTION 4 — The operation theatre]**

**Theatre list.**  
Today's theatre, by theatre. A booking is clash-checked — the theatre is
either free for that length of time or it is not.

**[SECTION 5 — The insurance desk]**

**Claims.**  
Every claim this hospital holds, sorted by what needs doing next. A
query to answer first; then an unsubmitted pre-auth; then an unsettled
submission; then the done pile.

**One claim.**  
Pre-auth amount, the TPA, the documents a payer needs, queries, the
settled amount, the deductions. The hospital's own record of every
case — the filing itself still happens on the payer's portal.

**Ageing.**  
What payers still owe, by how long they have owed it. The thirty-to-sixty
and over-ninety bars carry the money claims chase.

**Closing the counter.**  
End of shift. The cashier counts the drawer against what the counter
expected. A mismatch needs a note and goes on the audit log.

**[OUTRO]**  
That is the Hospital plan. For a multi-specialty, the top plan carries
this, plus the government-scheme desk, radiology, nursing stations and
the specialty records engine.
