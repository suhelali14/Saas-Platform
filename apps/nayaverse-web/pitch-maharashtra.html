Today 03 / 16

A patient's record stops at the door of the facility that wrote it. For every patient.

Chronic care
The diabetic on monthly review.
Every visit starts from zero. Last month's sugar reading and dose sit in a register at another PHC.
Mother and child
The pregnant woman, the newborn.
Antenatal notes don't reach the hospital where she delivers. Immunisation cards get lost.
Emergency
The accident case at the District Hospital.
Arrives with no allergy record, no list of current medicines, no history.
Elderly
The senior on BP and heart medicines.
Three doctors adjust the dose. None of them sees what the others wrote.
Long treatment
The TB patient on a six-month course.
History split between the PHC, the CHC and a separate programme register.
The district
The District Health Officer.
Sees last month's figures, collected by hand, weeks after the fact.
It lives on paper, inside the facility that wrote it. The next doctor starts from the complaint, not from the record.
The gap Nayaversse closes
The vision 04 / 16
Every citizen's health record follows them — from the first PHC visit, in Marathi, on their phone, forever.
For the citizen
One ABHA ID from the first visit.
No more re-telling your history at every counter.
For the clinician
A single screen; the previous visit beside today's.
Decisions off data, not off memory.
For the state
Live district data, and compliance built in.
PRAGATI reporting without a separate collection drive.
What Nayaversse is 05 / 16

One Indian-built product. Three pillars, one architecture.

01. Clinical
EMR, OPD triage queue, prescriptions, pharmacy with first-expiry dispensing, lab orders, discharge.
The system a doctor uses during every visit. Works on a basic laptop or a tablet.
02. Citizen
ABHA on first visit, queue position on their phone, prescription in Marathi, their history, forever.
The patient-facing side runs in twelve Indian languages, Marathi default.
03. State
District-level dashboards, PRAGATI metrics, MJPJAY claim submission, audit trail for DPDP.
The layer the state sees. Data is current because it comes from the same system clinicians already use.

For the PHC 08 / 16

Paper registers replaced with one screen, Marathi default.

Receptionist
Register, triage, print the OPD slip. ~25 seconds per patient.
Doctor
Case sheet, previous visit side-by-side, prescription from the drug catalogue.
Pharmacy
First-expiry dispensing. Expired stock never dispensed; near-expiry flagged.
In-charge
Daily close in two taps. Monthly report ready without a separate collection round.
For the state 09 / 16

Live district data. Compliance built in.

Because every PHC runs on the same system, the state does not run a separate monthly data-collection drive. The numbers on the district dashboard are the numbers at the counter.

DPDP Act 2023, NABH audit obligations and ABDM alignment are not a parallel compliance project; they are properties of the software itself.

Why Nayaversse 10 / 16

Three things no competitor in this room can match.

One

Marathi as a first-class language.

Not translated afterwards; designed for it. Staff screens, prescriptions, patient phone, in Marathi by default.
Two

One database per facility.

A breach in one PHC can never reach the records of any other. The architecture refuses the shared-tenancy risk other vendors live with.
Three

Honest about status.

Every claim we make on our chart is marked Live, Building or Waitlisted. The build fails if the words don't match reality.
